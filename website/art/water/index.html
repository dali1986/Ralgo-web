Every 60–90 seconds, three to five seas gather into a broad spiral, interweave and open again. Their voices pass a melody between them, meet in a richer harmony and settle as the water releases. Calling a confluence lets you begin one yourself; an unfolding encounter finishes before another begins.

Press a number while viewing the painting. A new cue makes a brief handover on its own track; fish, weather, sea transformations and colour changes can overlap. If paused, press Space to let your event unfold. H opens this guide; Escape closes it. Events still arrive naturally between your interventions.

Choose the material

Original restores the painting’s original mixture of sea finishes, with its fine flowing patterns and seed-chosen materials.

Oil / impasto carries the fine flowing sea patterns through thick, overlapping brush marks, raised paint edges, bristle grooves and small catches of oily light. Impressionist / pointillist builds the seas from separate, clustered dabs of bright colour, keeping fine crests and edges clear within the marks.

Watercolour gathers translucent washes into blooms, granulation and pooled edges. Ink on paper follows the currents in calligraphic brush strokes: broad ink curves taper into hairlines, with lifted ends and dry-brush gaps on fine paper.

Chalk on board draws mainly in white chalk on black, with broken dusty marks and occasional coloured passages. Collage carries continuous currents across torn newsprint, ribbed cardboard, woven cloth, crumpled foil, magazine pieces, tissue and painted rag paper. Their textures give way to the finest flowing contours.

White board / coloured pens follows the currents in fine coloured marker lines on a clean white board, with chisel-nib variation, thin outlines and darker overlapping ink.

Pencil sketch traces the seas in fine graphite, with varied pencil pressure, light shading and broken cross-hatching on softly grained paper. Fish become small outlined sketches; rain and ripples leave graphite marks.

Choose above the painting or press P to cycle through all nine styles. On a phone, use the Style menu. Switching keeps your seas, their currents and your stirring, even while paused. Your choice is remembered on this device and carried into sculptures, fish, rain and captures. H opens the full keyboard guide.

A sculpture made of seas

Three sheets of live paint lift from the water, curl through one another and turn in the light. Their fine patterns keep moving across both faces. Twenty seconds later, the knot melts back into the painting and leaves small currents behind.

You can still touch and stir the raised paint. The viewpoint holds during the lift; water and shoals continue below. Screenshots and videos keep the sculpture, too.

Sculpture is off by default. L or the Sculpture button enables it, then K begins a sculpture. Off removes an unfolding sculpture immediately, even while paused. Your explicit choice is remembered on this device. When enabled, sculptures also arrive naturally, a few minutes apart.

Closer to the extraordinary

Occasionally, a meeting of seas becomes opalescent enamel, threads of liquid gold or living stained glass. The finish travels along the sea’s contours, then dissolves back into paint. These rare transformations arrive a few minutes apart.

The camera sometimes approaches a meeting of pigments and currents, lingers among its finer engravings, then returns to the whole painting. A journey takes about 45 seconds. The water continues evolving beneath you.

Press T to call a rare finish or J to take a journey. C holds the current viewpoint and resumes it. Turning automatic journeys off brings the camera gently back to the whole painting. Pause holds both the water and the journey.

Music in the water

Choose Sound on, or press A. Seas trade short melodies over warm major and minor chords. Every pitched event follows the same harmony, and common notes connect one chord to the next. Water plucks, felt keys, wooden mallets, harp, porcelain and glass take turns. Each seed has its own score. Notes ring out and leave space; there are no drones.

Fish dart in little musical runs, lights sparkle in glass, and rain falls in separate droplets. Startled fish answer with brighter, shorter notes. Skipping stones tap across the stereo field. Colour cascades answer with rising arpeggios; transforming seas change instrument. A confluence brings its participating seas into a shared musical passage. Your taps and drags play the water, too.

Sound starts only when you ask for it. Pause holds the music with the painting; returning to a hidden tab picks up where you left off. Tide speed changes the water without rushing the music.

Three to fifty seas meet in a single painting. Each brings its own colours, shapes and current. Their waves exchange energy. A common current carries their pigments through one another, stretches their shapes, and winds them into shared vortices.

Colours come from 144 palettes, pairing electric blues with poppy red, peacock turquoise with citron, and molten coral with glacial light. Playful meetings include bubblegum lagoons, raspberry lemonade, mango disco and apricot fizz. Coloured crests bring out the brightest hues. Gossamer seas thread through silk, oil and heavy impasto, each with its own weight in the current. Even the broadest crests keep their fine etched patterns.

Eleven seas is the most frequent meeting. A smooth probability curve tapers towards three and fifty, making both ends rare. Each sea keeps a past of its own.

Eighteen sea structures range from braided tides and scalloped terraces to prismatic shoals, feather currents, cellular surf and folded marble. Keep the Original finish, or choose oil / impasto, impressionist / pointillist, watercolour, ink on paper, chalk on board, collage, white board / coloured pens or pencil sketch to reinterpret the same meeting. The selected medium carries the sea’s structure and fine engraving through its own brushwork.

Each sea has its own wave and sediment field: deposits bend later waves, while energetic water wears them away. A seed chooses the complete meeting, with a varied cast of structures, palettes and textures. Whirlpools can press against ink reefs, luminous lace, or fractured mineral plates. The painting follows the evolving mixture: open forms reveal the water beneath, and interference catches light inside the flow. The viewpoint drifts with a slow pan, a slight turn and a gentle change of scale. Hold it still with the camera control. Change the tide speed, or reveal the sediment histories with the layers control.

A random sea transforms every 6–20 seconds, and a colour cascade begins every 30 seconds. Shoals visit often, from small groups of large ribbon fish to dense clouds of tiny minnows: 10–160 fish, with silver, gold, coral stripes, jewel spots, neon, ink-and-pearl or festival colours. Lights, rain and skipping stones arrive between them. Fish, colour, structure and weather can unfold together. The intervals count active playback time and keep their pace when the tide speed changes; pausing holds everything still. Rain lands in brief crowns and airborne droplets, sending out bright, widening rings. Skipping stones throw forward sprays; their hops and splashes grow smaller as they lose energy. Rain, fish and stones leave real disturbances in the water, while colour and shape changes carry its history forward.
